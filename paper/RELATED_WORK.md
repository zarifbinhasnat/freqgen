# Related Work

Organised into four groups that map directly onto freqgen's argument:
(1) the spectral fingerprint exists, (2) detectors that exploit it, (3) attacks
that try to erase it, (4) the theoretical foundation. Each entry has the
positioning note that explains how it relates to our contribution.

---

## 1. The spectral fingerprint: real vs AI-generated images differ in frequency

These papers establish the core observation that freqgen starts from.

### 1.1 Fourier Spectrum Discrepancies in Deep Network Generated Images
**Dzanic, Shah, Witherden — NeurIPS 2020**
arXiv: [1911.06465](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06465)

The seminal paper showing that GAN/VAE outputs systematically under-produce
high-frequency Fourier components relative to real images, achieving 99.2%
detection accuracy from Fourier features alone. Also proposes making generated
images "more closely match authentic ones in the frequency domain" — the
earliest discussion of what we call the spectral matching direction.

**Positioning:** Establishes the high-frequency deficit for GANs. freqgen
extends this finding to diffusion models and formalises it as a radial profile
gap (our ~21× high-band gap), then exploits it in the reverse direction
(attack, not detection).

---

### 1.2 Leveraging Frequency Analysis for Deep Fake Image Recognition
**Frank, Eisenhofer, Schönherr, Fischer, Kolossa, Holz — ICML 2020**
arXiv: [2003.08685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08685)

Demonstrates that GAN upsampling operations leave periodic grid artifacts
detectable as sharp 2-D peaks in the Fourier magnitude (not just radial
averages). Builds a classifier on frequency-domain features.

**Positioning:** Directly motivates our **residual detector** (`R = |FFT| /
ring-mean`) which captures exactly these 2-D peaks. The paper shows these peaks
survive radial normalisation — the same reason our residual detector evades the
attack while the radial one does not.

---

### 1.3 Intriguing Properties of Synthetic Images: From GANs to Diffusion Models
**Corvi, Cozzolino, Poggi, Nagano, Verdoliva — arXiv 2023**
arXiv: [2304.06408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06408)

Extends spectral analysis to diffusion models, finding "significant differences
in the mid-high frequency signal content, observable in radial and angular
spectral power distributions." Compares radial spectra of real vs generated
images across multiple architectures.

**Positioning:** The closest prior work to freqgen's Notebook 1. We use their
radial-profile methodology and band decomposition (low/mid/high). Our
contribution adds the quantitative gap measurement on Synthbuster data,
the matching attack, and the evasion experiment.

---

### 1.4 Synthbuster: Towards Detection of Diffusion Model Generated Images
**Bammey — IEEE Open Journal of Signal Processing, 2023**
Dataset: [Zenodo 10066460](https://zenodo.org/records/10066460)

Introduces the Synthbuster dataset (9,000 images, 9 generators, scene-paired
with RAISE-1k reals) and a detector based on inherent frequency artifacts left
by the diffusion process.

**Positioning:** freqgen uses the Synthbuster dataset for its real-data
experiments. The paper's detector is a hand-crafted frequency-based baseline
analogous to our radial logistic regression.

---

### 1.5 Leveraging Natural Frequency Deviation for Diffusion-Generated Image Detection (DEFEND)
**Zhang, Zhang, Ge, Süsstrunk — ICLR 2025**
OpenReview: [fPBExgC1m9](https://openreview.net/forum?id=fPBExgC1m9)

Proposes applying a weighted filter to the Fourier spectrum, suppressing less
discriminative bands while enhancing informative ones. Exploits that diffusion
images show "progressively larger differences from real images across
low-to-high-frequency bands." Robust against common online perturbations.

**Positioning:** A concurrent ICLR 2025 paper using a related frequency-band
weighting idea. Key difference: DEFEND is a detection-only method (trains a
weighted spectral classifier); freqgen's matching attack shows that any such
band-energy detector can be defeated by the same FFT inversion it relies on.

## 2. Learned spectral detectors — the SOTA we test against

### 2.1 On the Detection of Synthetic Images Generated by Diffusion Models
**Corvi, Cozzolino, Zingarini, Poggi, Nagano, Verdoliva — ICASSP 2023**
GitHub: [grip-unina/DMimageDetection](https://github.com/grip-unina/DMimageDetection)

Provides the training dataset used by SPAI (180k real + 180k LDM fakes,
COCO/LSUN reals). Studies how GAN-trained detectors transfer to diffusion
models; shows limited generalisation. The dataset benchmark for subsequent work.

**Positioning:** Training data source for SPAI. freqgen adopts the Synthbuster
eval set instead of this training split.

---

### 2.2 DIRE: Diffusion-Generated Image Detection via Reconstruction Error
**Wang, Bao, Zhou, Wang, Hu, Chen, Li — ICCV 2023**
arXiv: [2303.09295](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09295)
GitHub: [ZhendongWang6/DIRE](https://github.com/ZhendongWang6/DIRE)

Measures the pixel-domain reconstruction error of a pre-trained diffusion model
on the input image. Real images have high reconstruction error (the model
struggles to reproduce them); fakes have low error (the model knows them).

**Positioning:** DIRE works in pixel space, not frequency. Conceptually parallel
to SPAI's SRS (which works in frequency/latent space). Both model the
distribution of real images; both would be tested against our attack.

---

### 2.3 SPAI: Any-Resolution AI-Generated Image Detection by Spectral Learning
**Karageorgiou, Papadopoulos, Kompatsiaris, Gavves — CVPR 2025**
arXiv: [2411.19417](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19417)
GitHub: [mever-team/spai](https://github.com/mever-team/spai)

The current SOTA. Uses a frozen ViT-B/16 (MFM pre-trained) to learn a spectral
model of real images. Introduces SRS (cosine similarity between latent
representations of original / low-pass / high-pass versions across 12 ViT
blocks) and SCA (patch-level attention for any-resolution). +5.5% AUC over
prior SOTA across 13 generators.

**Positioning:** The primary detector freqgen attacks. freqgen's central
question: does the radial matching attack (O(1 FFT)) defeat a learned ViT-based
spectral detector? The answer determines the paper's framing (attack finding or
evaluation tool).

---

### 2.4 FIRE: Robust Detection of Diffusion-Generated Images via Frequency-Guided Reconstruction Error
**Chu, Xu, Wang, Zhang, You, Zhou — CVPR 2025**
arXiv: [2412.07140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07140)

Observes that diffusion models struggle to reconstruct mid-band frequency
information from real images. Uses the variation in reconstruction error
before/after frequency decomposition as the detection signal. Generalises to
unseen diffusion models and is robust to common perturbations.

**Positioning:** Another CVPR 2025 concurrent work using frequency + reconstruction
error. Complements SPAI. freqgen's residual detector (`R = |FFT| / ring-mean`)
is a hand-crafted approximation of the within-ring structure FIRE exploits.

---

## 3. Attacks on spectral detectors — where freqgen sits

### 3.1 StealthDiffusion: Towards Evading Diffusion Forensic Detection
**Zhou, Sun, Chen, Kuang, Sun, Ji — ACM MM 2024**
arXiv: [2408.05669](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05669)

Proposes an adversarial framework that uses stable diffusion's latent space
to modify AI-generated images into imperceptible adversarial examples that
evade forensic detectors. Control-VAE reduces spectral differences between
fakes and reals in the process.

**Positioning:** The closest prior work to freqgen's attack. Key difference:
StealthDiffusion requires a full diffusion model inference loop (GPU, ~seconds
per image, adversarial optimisation). freqgen's radial matching is a **single
FFT pass** (CPU, milliseconds), yet achieves the same spectral gap closure.
This efficiency gap is the novel contribution. StealthDiffusion is the
strongest known prior attack; we should compare evasion rates.

## 4. Theoretical foundation: natural image statistics

### 4.1 The 1/f² Power Law of Natural Images
**Ruderman & Bialek (1994); Field (1987); van der Schaaf & van Hateren (1996)**
Reference: Field, D.J. (1987) *Relations between the statistics of natural
images and the response properties of cortical cells.* JOSA A, 4(12).

Natural images exhibit a power spectrum decaying as P(f) ∝ f^−α with α ≈ 2
(equivalently P(r) ∝ r^−1 in magnitude). This is the baseline our `spectral_report`
slope ≈ −1 (magnitude) maps to, and why AI fakes with slope ≈ −2 (steeper than
real) are anomalous. The 1/f law is the theoretical reason why the high-band gap
is detectable.
